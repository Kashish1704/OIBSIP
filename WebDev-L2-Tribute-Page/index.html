01 — THE STORY

A mind that
saw beyond
the ordinary.

Born in Erode, Tamil Nadu, Ramanujan grew up with a deep fascination for mathematics. Numbers were never just symbols to him — they were a language waiting to be understood.

With very little formal training in advanced mathematics, he explored complex ideas independently, filling notebooks with original results. His determination eventually brought his work to the attention of the Cambridge mathematician G. H. Hardy.

In 1914, Ramanujan travelled to Cambridge. The collaboration that followed produced remarkable work, even as he faced serious health challenges far from home.

A NUMBER THAT BECAME A LEGEND
1729
1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³

Known as the Hardy–Ramanujan number, 1729 is the smallest positive integer expressible as the sum of two positive cubes in two different ways.

03 — A LIFE IN MOMENTS

Brief in years.
Infinite in impact.

1887

A beginning in Erode

Srinivasa Ramanujan is born on 22 December in Erode, in present-day Tamil Nadu.

1903

The spark deepens

A mathematics book by G. S. Carr helps shape his independent mathematical exploration.

1913

A letter to Cambridge

He writes to G. H. Hardy, sharing mathematical results that leave Hardy astonished.

1914

A new chapter

Ramanujan travels to England and begins working at the University of Cambridge.

1918

Recognition

He is elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and a Fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge.

1920

A lasting legacy

He passes away on 26 April, aged 32, leaving behind ideas that mathematicians continue to study.
